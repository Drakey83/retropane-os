## The design (rule 0)

The ruling: draw the menu over the screen as shown, Today's pixels under
the glass copied, Today's commands not run again under it. Reading the
renderer first changed how: the menu's glass is a panel, and a panel's
body starts from its clears, the wallpaper itself (`rp_draw::panel_cmds`,
ADR-0023 rule 3, the frosted glass over the photo), so **nothing Today
draws shows under the glass body at all**; Today's pixels show only
through the panel's four rounded corners. So the renderer now finds, in
each dirty rectangle, the panel that clears the most of it, and draws the
commands before that panel (and copies the background) only into the
pieces it leaves: the four corners and anything of the rectangle outside
it. The panel and everything after it are drawn as before.

- **Windows Mobile's GWES** clips a window's paint by the windows over it
  (the visible region), but repaints the window under a translucent one;
  **Android and Flutter** skip what an opaque layer hides (overdraw
  avoidance) and draw everything under a translucent one.
- **Ours** knows this glass is translucent yet hides what is under it:
  its translucency shows the wallpaper, not the screen. So it skips every
  command under the body, translucent glass included, and draws the
  corners exactly as before.
- **Why this beats copying Today's pixels:** a copy of the picture on
  screen would need that picture to be exactly Today's list (true only
  when nothing under the menu changed that frame), and it would dither
  Today's 16-bit pixels twice. Drawing the four corners needs neither: the
  pixels are the reference's (pixel-checked), on every screen and every
  panel, not only the Start menu. The corners are 4 x 12 x 12 = 576 px (the shell's panels'
  radius, panel_art.rs), against the menu's 151,200.
